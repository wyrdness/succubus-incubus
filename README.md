## Overview

The succubus and incubus are complementary demons appearing prominently in medieval Christian demonology. A succubus takes female form to seduce men during sleep, while the incubus takes male form to assault women. Repeated nocturnal encounters were believed to drain the victim's physical and mental health, potentially causing death. According to the Malleus Maleficarum (1486), succubi collect semen from seduced men; male demons (incubi) then use this semen to impregnate human females. The offspring, called cambions, were said to be born deformed or supernaturally influenced. The succubus typically manifests as a beautiful woman, though closer inspection might reveal deformities such as bird-like claws or a serpentine tail. King James I of England, in his Daemonologie (1597), theorized that demons employed two methods: stealing sperm from corpses or possessing dead bodies for sexual relations. Modern scholarship attributes the experiences underlying these legends primarily to sleep paralysis with hypnagogic or hypnopompic hallucinations, which can produce vivid sensations of a presence, physical pressure, and even sexual experiences during the hypnagogic state. The phenomenon appears in folklore worldwide under various names, including the Mara of Germanic tradition, the Alp of Central Europe, and the Mare of Old English texts.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Late Latin succuba, from succubare (to lie beneath), combined with the prefix sub- and cubare (to lie down). The incubus derives from incubare (to lie upon). Both terms entered medieval theological Latin.
- **Meaning**: Succubus: 'she who lies beneath'; Incubus: 'he who lies upon'
- **First use**: Succubus in English: late 14th century. Both terms widely used in medieval Latin theological writing from the 13th century onward.

## Physical Description

- **Morphology**: The succubus presents as a beautiful human woman; the incubus as a handsome or powerfully built man. Both may conceal monstrous features beneath their attractive exteriors. Medieval art occasionally depicted them with wings, claws, or tails. The Malleus Maleficarum describes their ability to change gender and assume the form of a deceased loved one.

## Distribution

The succubus/incubus tradition is found throughout medieval Europe and has functional analogues in cultures worldwide. The specific Christian demonological framework is primarily European (especially Western and Central Europe), but similar nocturnal sexual demon traditions appear across the Middle East, Africa, and Asia.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Literal demonic visitation** _(Medieval Catholic theologians, Heinrich Kramer, Jacob Sprenger)_

Traditional theological view: succubi and incubi are real demons sent by Satan to corrupt human souls through sexual sin, as described in medieval demonological literature.

### Scientific

**Sleep paralysis with hypnagogic hallucination** _(Sleep researchers broadly, David Hufford (The Terror That Comes in the Night, 1982))_

The standard modern explanation: during sleep paralysis, the brain remains in REM state while the body is temporarily immobilized. Hypnagogic or hypnopompic hallucinations can produce vivid sensations of presence, physical pressure, and sexual experiences, explaining reports across cultures.

### Skeptical

**Cultural projection and clerical anxiety** _(Historians of religion broadly, Walter Stephens)_

The succubus/incubus tradition reflects the sexual anxieties of celibate clerical culture; nocturnal emissions and sexual dreams required theological explanation, and the demon framework served social functions around sin, guilt, and bodily control.
